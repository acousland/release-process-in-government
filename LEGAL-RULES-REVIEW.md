# Legal rules review

Reviewed 23 November 2026. This is a software and source interpretation review of the release routes implemented in the questionnaire. Internal delegations, actual entity registrations, case facts, source restrictions, and legal judgments must be verified by the responsible agency team.

## User experience

Users answer factual questions. They are never required to enter an Act, section, or legal justification. The authority record and authorityCheck question have been removed. A departmental function with no established statutory disclosure route produces a referral identifying the unresolved authority, without requiring the user to solve it.

## Logical model

Required conditions use three values: met, failed, and unconfirmed. AND fails when any operand fails and succeeds only when all operands succeed. OR succeeds when any operand succeeds and fails only when all operands fail. Unknown inputs remain unconfirmed. `legal-conditions.mjs` supplies conditions to the findings, final outcome, live tracker, and export. Privacy alternatives are evaluated by the shared `privacyGrounds` function. Purpose selection alone cannot establish a specialist sharing basis.

## Deliberate boundaries and verification limits

This review covers the mapped release routes and reference checks above. The General Order's 626-title index is a source-navigation index. Those 626 Acts have not each been converted into executable disclosure rules. Some professional determinations remain bundled in plain-language confirmation questions, including participant roles, scheme exclusion screening, particular youth justice roles, current delegations, transfer grounds, and program-specific source restrictions. The tool must continue to show unresolved checks, rather than claim a complete legal determination. VDS data-holdings notices, CDO onward disclosure, and specialised CIP workflows require their dedicated statutory processes.

No software test can establish the truth of a case fact, internal instrument, or professional legal judgment. Regression checks cover alternative paths, missing facts, failed conditions, privacy AND/OR boundaries, the form/map correspondence, and agreement between the tracker and release record.
